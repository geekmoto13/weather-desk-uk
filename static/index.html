A PATH FROM YOUR STATION

Radio prediction setup

Your transmitting location comes from the dashboard’s saved coordinates, rounded to approximately 1 km. The model sends this approximate location and the settings below to KC2G. These predictions use idealised fixed-gain antennas at both ends.

SNR is predicted in the selected mode’s bandwidth. It is not a measured on-air report. The model’s reference frequencies represent bands; they are not operating-frequency or licence advice. The lowest usable band means the lowest tested frequency at 50% or greater circuit reliability. A true continuous LUF needs a fuller frequency analysis.

Choose your location

UK postcode, town, or a 4-, 6- or 8-character Maidenhead grid square. UK and Republic of Ireland locations select the appropriate observations, climate reference, forecast and warnings. Irish locations use Met Éireann in Automatic mode.

Location lookup: Postcodes.io / Open-Meteo / GeoNames.

Automatic keeps your connected Ambient station first. Every public station must report both a recent usable temperature and measured rainfall. A reported zero counts; missing or rejected rain data does not. The nearest qualifying CWOP or configured Weather Underground station within 75 km is preferred, with a rain-reporting official station as fallback. Reports are cached. If none qualifies, the dashboard says so and retries. Station name, distance, observation time and reported rain period are shown. Rolling totals are never added into daily or yearly counters.

Official-station mode uses a real nearby airport observation, identified by station name and distance. A numerical precipitation field is required; rain weather codes alone do not qualify. Solar and daily max gust may be absent. The rainfall ledger uses a separate nearby Environment Agency gauge in England where available, labelled with its distance and coverage. The daily climate archive can lag several weeks. Calendar days use your connected Ambient station where available; otherwise the named archive station.

About the UK & Irish readings

Lightning: nearby flash positions and occurrence times come from EUMETSAT Meteosat LI Level-2 Lightning Flashes, collection EO:EUM:DAT:0691. Recent observations are cached to speed subsequent loads. Missing values and quality flags are checked before displaying a flash. Distances and compass directions use your saved coordinates, not a remote public weather station. The closest accepted flash is selected within 75 km during the 45-minute window ending at the displayed satellite data time. A partial load says nearest loaded flash; complete, fresh, gap-free coverage is required before showing No detections. Quality warnings, failed downloads and stale data are not all-clear messages. Satellite flashes include cloud lightning and do not identify an exact ground-strike location. The Blitzortung live map is a separate source and can show different activity. It uses the provider’s documented iframe, not its restricted raw feed. EUMETSAT consumer credentials use the same local Windows account protection/private-file permissions as Ambient, in a separate file. OAuth tokens stay in memory and are renewed automatically. The catalogue is checked every five minutes; its archive comes in ten-minute chunks. Data beyond 30 minutes old are marked delayed, and the actual data time is always shown. Contains modified EUMETSAT Meteosat data, 2026, under CC BY 4.0. The earlier Weatherbit connection is no longer used, and no paid Weatherbit requests are made. Map and decoder libraries are bundled or linked with their attribution. NOAA GLM · EUMETSAT LI.

Republic of Ireland: Location selection switches Automatic public weather to Met Éireann EDR rain-reporting stations, the Met Éireann point forecast and all Republic of Ireland land warnings. Northern Ireland keeps its UK sources. Observations are provisional; dew point and feels-like values are calculated. The EDR pressure is station pressure, not sea-level pressure. Rain totals use UTC observing days; unavailable months or years are not inferred from a short history. Irish daily records and calculated 1991–2020 averages use the named Met Éireann station; estimated temperatures, estimated rain and nonzero trace/sum flags are excluded. Some reference dates lack enough baseline years and remain unavailable. The archive is updated monthly and its actual latest date is shown. Radar remains RainViewer, separately credited. A connected Ambient station remains your observation source in Automatic mode.

Met Éireann attribution: Copyright Met Éireann. Source www.met.ie. Data published under CC BY 4.0; the forecast is subject to the Met Éireann custom open-data terms, including displaying warnings. Units, storage format and calculated references have been modified from the originals. Met Éireann does not accept liability for errors, omissions, availability, or loss arising from use of its data.

Location boundary: Republic of Ireland boundary © OpenStreetMap contributors, via Wambacher / geoBoundaries, ODbL 1.0. This packaged boundary is for weather-provider routing; use a precise location near the border.

Station readings: Automatic uses your connected Ambient Weather station, otherwise the nearest usable CWOP report via NOAA MADIS, with a nearby rain-reporting airport as fallback via the NOAA Aviation Weather Center global METAR feed. Every selected public station must report both a recent usable temperature and a measured precipitation amount. An explicit zero qualifies; missing values, rejected rain data and rain weather codes alone do not. If none qualifies, no temperature-only station is selected. The station’s own rain is labelled with its reporting period. Airport observations are real measurements, with their actual time and distance shown. Missing sensors remain unavailable. In England, a separately named Environment Agency rain gauge supplies measured rainfall where one is found within 30 km. A ≥ subtotal has gaps; unchecked observations are provisional. Day/week/month/year sums all use the same 15-minute gauge intervals and local calendar boundaries. The equivalent rate is the last 15-minute amount multiplied by four. It is not an airport or home-gauge measurement. Airport pressure is labelled QNH when it is an altimeter setting. An Ambient station uses calibrated relative pressure. The pressure arrow compares readings about three hours apart.

Forecast beacon: Open-Meteo supplies DWD ICON hourly model forecasts for your selected UK coordinates. This is a forecast, separate from your observed station. It is refreshed about every fifteen minutes and labelled with retrieval time, since the feed does not supply the original model issue time. The prevailing condition over the next twelve hours sets the beacon: steady blue clear, flashing blue clouds, steady red rain, flashing red wintry precipitation. Missing/stale data leaves it unlit.

Daily climate: records and calculated 1991–2020 averages use the named UK station in NOAA NCEI GHCN-Daily, including ECA&D-sourced observations. They are archive extrema, not certified UK records or published Met Office normals. We average each calendar date with at least 24 of 30 valid baseline years (7 of 8 leap days); flagged/missing values are excluded. Climate observing days may differ from your home station’s midnight-to-midnight day. Archive observations can lag several weeks; its latest date is shown. Rain normals, MTD/YTD records and station totals use matching calendar-date windows. Missing historic days exclude that year’s rain total.

Calendar: completed daily mean = (high + low) ÷ 2. Green is within ±1°F (about ±0.6°C) of the calculated daily average; red is warmer, blue colder. The band is this app’s display choice, not an official tolerance. Today, missing days and incomplete personal-station days remain uncoloured. Completed station days are cached locally.

MIDAS Open: the optional history explorer imports quality-version-1 Met Office daily-temperature and daily-rain CSVs downloaded from CEDA. Its dates explicitly mean 24 hours ending at 09:00 UTC; they are kept separate from the NOAA daily comparisons. Suspect/estimated, multi-day, 12-hour and unsupported records are excluded. Imported history and coverage are saved locally. No MIDAS observations are bundled. Source: Met Office MIDAS Open v202607, CEDA, Open Government Licence v3.0.

Met Office monthly archive: the separate monthly card uses the nearest open station in the Met Office’s public historic station archive. It shows full-month summaries and averages calculated from 1991–2020 monthly data. Its station may differ from the daily archive station. Estimated and provisional data are marked. Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

Radar: past precipitation radar by RainViewer, with visible RainViewer and Natural Earth attribution. It refreshes every five minutes, offers six past frames and stays within native zoom 7. Colours show reflectivity, not an exact surface rainfall amount. Missing tiles can make the map incomplete. The Met Office map link opens its official radar page. This personal-use edition uses RainViewer’s personal/educational API and Open-Meteo’s noncommercial free API.

Warnings: Met Office Weather Warnings are filtered to your point using the official public ArcGIS polygons. This is a public beta feed; the Met Office does not support this API. We check every minute, label failures and hide operational warning content once a successful check is over three minutes old. Always use the official warning map for the latest position. © Crown copyright 2026, Met Office. Outside the UK: © British Crown copyright 2026, Met Office.

Time delays between this website and the metoffice.gov.uk domain are possible. For the most up-to-date awareness information as published by the Met Office, please refer to https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/.

MET OFFICE · MIDAS OPEN

Your saved UK climate history

Start with the import instructions below. NOAA daily comparisons and the Met Office monthly card remain available while no MIDAS files are imported.

Import MIDAS files · one-time setup
  1. Create a free CEDA account and open the daily temperature or daily rainfall dataset.
  2. Under Explore, choose a station and qc-version-1. Download its yearly observation CSVs. For a 1991–2020 average, import those 30 years. Nearby Watnall is in Nottinghamshire, source ID 00556. Rainfall coverage may be much shorter than temperature coverage.
  3. Select the downloaded CSVs together, or a ZIP containing them, then import. Files remain saved for the next startup. Repeat with additional years when needed.


Up to 300 files and 12 MB per import. Import observation CSVs only; metadata-only files are not observations. CEDA credentials are used on CEDA's website. This dashboard does not request or store them.

Coastal tide observations

The curve shows the past 24 hours of measured, 15-minute mean coastal water levels, with gaps left blank. Heights are metres relative to Ordnance Datum Newlyn (m AOD). They are not heights above Chart Datum. Negative values are normal.

These observations do not predict the next high or low tide. Use the official Admiralty EasyTide link for future tide times and its own height datum.
